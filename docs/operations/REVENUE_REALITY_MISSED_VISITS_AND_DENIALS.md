# Revenue Reality: Missed Visits, Denials, and BCBA Earnings

Last updated: 2026-07-05

The earlier 10/15-client revenue estimate may look low because it used conservative assumptions: 15 direct RBT/therapy hours per client per week, Medicaid-heavy collected rates, missed visits, and claims loss. ABA revenue can be much higher with more authorized hours, better commercial rates, high attendance, and low denial rates. It can also collapse quickly if clients miss visits or claims are denied.

The revised model separates weak center-style operations from a software-style operating model. In a strong model, parent expectations, RBT supervision, schedule adherence, data quality, claim follow-up, and reauthorization are managed daily. If 25 authorized direct RBT/therapy hours/client/week are clinically appropriate and actually delivered, the revenue picture is much stronger than the earlier conservative model.

Important clarification: direct therapy hours/client/week are not BCBA hours/client/week. BCBA time is modeled separately as supervision, protocol modification, assessment, treatment planning, and parent training. In many practical models, BCBA involvement is about 2-3 hours/client/week, but the exact amount depends on clinical need, authorization, state rules, and payer rules.

## Why Revenue May Look Too Low

Revenue depends on:

- Authorized hours per client.
- Actually attended hours.
- Payer rate.
- BCBA billable supervision/protocol hours.
- Denial rate.
- Timely note completion.
- RBT staffing.
- Parent cancellation/no-show rate.
- Credentialing effective dates.
- Prior authorization units remaining.

If a client has 25-30 authorized direct hours/week and the payer pays more than Medicaid, revenue is much higher. If the child attends only 70-80% of sessions, or claims are denied, revenue drops sharply.

## Revised High-Utilization P&L View

Using 25 direct hours/client/week, strong attendance, clean claims, and India contractor support:

| Clients | Collected Revenue/Month | India Admin/Software Cost | Profit Before Owner Tax | Margin |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | about $88,000 | about $1,150/month | about $36,500/month | about 41% |
| 15 | about $126,000 | about $1,800/month | about $52,400/month | about 42% |

This is not guaranteed. It depends on actual payer rates, authorization terms, RBT staffing, state rules, attendance, and documentation quality. But it is a better model for the way you are describing the operation.

## If the Owner BCBA Works 40 Hours

A 40-hour owner BCBA week should be modeled as capacity, not guaranteed revenue. Even with India RCM and software support, the owner still needs non-billable clinical and business time.

| Client Model | Planned BCBA Billable Hrs/Wk | Non-Billable Owner Hrs/Wk | Practical Read |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 10 clients, conservative | 24-26 | 14-16 | Reasonable if claims and notes are clean |
| 10 clients, strong utilization | 28-30 | 10-12 | Good target after workflows stabilize |
| 15 clients, controlled | 32-34 | 6-8 | Upper range; requires strong RBTs and clean authorization workflows |
| 15 clients, aggressive | 36-38 | 2-4 | High risk; small crisis or denial spike can break the model |

If billing/admin is delegated, the owner can earn more because more time can become billable BCBA service. But the owner cannot delegate final clinical responsibility, high-risk review, treatment-plan integrity, and supervision compliance.

## Missed Visit Loss

Missed visits are one of the biggest risks.

Example:

- 10 clients.
- 20 direct hours/client/week.
- $65 collected/hour.
- Scheduled direct revenue: 10 x 20 x 4.33 x $65 = about $56,290/month before BCBA hours.
- If attendance is 90%, about $5,600/month of direct service opportunity is lost.
- If attendance is 80%, about $11,300/month is lost.

The app must track:

- Scheduled hours.
- Attended hours.
- Cancelled by family.
- Cancelled by staff.
- No-show.
- Make-up offered.
- Make-up completed.

For your operating style, missed visits should be treated like a preventable operations defect unless there is a valid clinical/family reason. The app should show attendance by client, RBT, parent, time of day, state, and payer. Repeated cancellations should trigger parent training, schedule revision, or discharge/escalation rules rather than being accepted as normal.

## Claim Denial Loss

If claims are submitted but not paid:

- 5% denial/loss on $60,000 collections risk = $3,000/month.
- 10% denial/loss = $6,000/month.
- 15% denial/loss = $9,000/month.

This is why claims follow-up and payer templates matter. A low-cost India RCM team saves money only if it prevents denials and speeds collections.

## What Does the BCBA Earn?

There are two different meanings:

1. Employee/contract BCBA market pay.
2. Owner BCBA earnings from profit plus any paid clinical hours.

For an owner BCBA, earnings come from:

- Billable BCBA supervision/protocol hours.
- Remaining business profit after RBT wages, taxes, rent, software, billing, insurance, credentialing, and admin.

At 10 clients, if the model is Medicaid-heavy and admin/rent is high, owner earnings may be disappointing. At 10 clients with better payer rates, high attendance, and low denials, owner earnings can improve substantially.

## Practical Rule

Before scaling:

- Do not count scheduled hours as revenue.
- Count only attended, documented, authorized, clean-claim hours.
- Track missed visit dollar loss weekly.
- Track denial dollar loss weekly.
- Track owner BCBA unpaid admin time.
- Track claim AR over 30 days.

If 10 clients are not producing predictable cash flow, 15 clients will create more work and risk, not necessarily more profit.
